# Sample Prompts for Messaging & Positioning

These examples show how to get the best results from the messaging-positioning skill. The more context you provide, the sharper the output.

---

## 1. Full Messaging Framework from Scratch

> We are building the messaging framework for Anvil, a contract lifecycle management platform for legal teams at companies with 200-2,000 employees. We compete with DocuSign CLM, Ironclad, and manual processes (Word docs + email chains). Our key differentiator is that we auto-extract obligations from signed contracts and create automated compliance workflows — something competitors require manual tagging for. Our buyers are General Counsels and VP Legal Ops. We have customer quotes from 8 win/loss interviews I can share. Build me the full messaging document.
>
> Additional context: We are Series B, 80 employees, $12M ARR. Our average deal size is $45K/year. We win most often against manual processes and lose most often to Ironclad. Our fastest-growing segment is regulated industries (fintech, healthcare SaaS) where compliance obligations are complex.

**Why this works:** Specifies the product, target buyer, named competitors, a claimed differentiator, and offers customer evidence. Includes company stage and win/loss patterns. This gives the skill enough to execute all six phases with confidence.

## 2. Repositioning for a New Market Segment

> We currently sell a data observability platform to data engineering teams at enterprises. Our messaging is very technical — "schema drift detection," "pipeline health monitoring," etc. We want to expand into selling to VP Analytics / Head of BI personas at mid-market companies (200-1,000 employees). These buyers care about data trust and dashboard reliability, not pipeline internals. Help me reposition our messaging for this new persona without alienating our existing technical buyer.
>
> Here is our current homepage headline: "End-to-End Data Pipeline Observability." Our current tagline is "See Everything. Fix Anything." Our sales team says prospects in the analytics persona ask "will this help me trust my dashboards?" within the first 5 minutes of every call. That feels like a signal.

**Why this works:** Clearly states the current positioning, the target shift, the new persona's priorities, and the constraint (do not alienate the existing buyer). Includes actual current copy and a sales insight. This is a repositioning exercise, not a from-scratch build.

## 3. Persona-Specific Messaging Cards

> I need sales-ready messaging cards for three personas who buy our employee engagement platform: CHRO (cares about retention and culture metrics), VP People Ops (cares about survey fatigue and actionable insights), and CFO (cares about cost of turnover and ROI). Our positioning is already set — we are "the only engagement platform that connects sentiment data to business outcomes in real time." I need each card to include their pain, our promise, 3 proof points, one objection handle, and a CTA. Output as a format sales can print on half-page cards.

**Why this works:** Positioning is already established — this is a Phase 4 request. Specifies the exact personas, what each cares about, the output format, and what each card should contain.

## 4. Messaging Audit of Existing Copy

> Here is our current homepage copy, sales deck headline slides, and the "About" section from our last press release. [paste content]
>
> Audit this messaging against best practices. Specifically, I want to know:
> - Which claims are undifferentiated (competitors say the same thing)?
> - Which language is insider jargon that buyers would not use?
> - Where are we leading with features instead of value?
> - What is missing that should be there?
> - Is our competitive differentiation clear or muddled?
>
> Then rewrite the homepage hero section (headline, subhead, supporting paragraph, CTA) and the sales deck opening slide using properly structured messaging. For the rewrite, our primary buyer is VP of Engineering at companies with 50-500 developers.

**Why this works:** Provides the actual content to audit. Asks for specific failure modes to check. Specifies the buyer for the rewrite. Requests rewritten output, not just critique.

## 5. Quick Positioning Statement

> I need a positioning statement for an API security platform that helps DevSecOps teams find and fix API vulnerabilities before deployment. Main competitors are Salt Security, Noname Security, and manual penetration testing. Our edge is that we integrate directly into the CI/CD pipeline and block vulnerable APIs from deploying — shift-left for API security. Target buyer is Head of Application Security at companies with 50+ microservices. Give me three positioning statement options and recommend the sharpest one.

**Why this works:** Provides enough context for a focused Phase 3 exercise without requiring the full six-phase workflow. Specifies competitors, differentiation, buyer, and the desired output format.

## Tips for Best Results

**Provide customer language whenever possible.** Paste quotes from win/loss interviews, G2 reviews, support tickets, or sales call transcripts. The skill will mirror buyer language, and real quotes are always better than assumptions. Even a few verbatim quotes dramatically improve the output.

**Name your competitors explicitly.** Do not say "we compete with several players in the space." Say "we compete with Salesforce, HubSpot, and the status quo of spreadsheets." Specific competitors enable specific differentiation. Include the status quo and "do nothing" as alternatives — these are often the real competition.

**State what you have already tried.** If your current messaging is underperforming, share it. Diagnosing what is broken is faster than starting from zero. Include current website copy, sales deck headlines, or email templates. The more raw material, the more precise the recommendations.

**Be honest about evidence gaps.** If you do not have customer metrics or proof points yet, say so. The skill will mark claims as "[NEEDS PROOF]" rather than fabricating evidence, and it will recommend validation methods to fill the gaps. This is far better than inflating claims that will fail under buyer scrutiny.

**Specify the output format.** "Full messaging document," "positioning one-pager," or "persona messaging cards" will get you precisely structured output. If you do not specify, the skill defaults to the full document. You can also request multiple formats — for example, "full document plus a positioning one-pager for the exec team."

**Share your company stage and resources.** A Series A startup with 20 customers needs different messaging than a Series D company with 500. The skill adapts its recommendations — including validation methods — based on what is realistic for your stage. Include your ARR, headcount, and growth goals if possible.

**Indicate your timeline.** If you need messaging for a specific launch, rebrand, or board meeting, say so. The skill will calibrate the depth of output and validation plan accordingly. A "we need this for a launch next week" request gets a different recommendation than "we are doing a foundational messaging exercise over the next quarter."

**Share win/loss patterns.** If you know who you win against and who you lose to, share that. "We win 70% of deals against manual processes but only 30% against Competitor X" is incredibly useful input. It tells the skill where your positioning is working and where it needs strengthening.

## What to Expect from Each Output Type

**Full Messaging Document** (request with: "build me a full messaging document" or "create a complete messaging framework"):
You will receive a 300-500 line document with all four hierarchy layers, differentiation stress-test, competitive response guide, approved/forbidden language, persona cards, and a validation plan. This is the most comprehensive output. Allow for follow-up iterations.

**Positioning One-Pager** (request with: "create a positioning one-pager" or "I need an executive summary of our positioning"):
You will receive a single-page-equivalent document with the positioning statement, target segment, 3-4 differentiators, competitive frame, and a condensed strategic narrative. Good for board decks or executive alignment.

**Persona Messaging Cards** (request with: "build persona messaging cards" or "I need sales-ready messaging for our buyer personas"):
You will receive one card per persona with pain, promise, proof, objection handle, and CTA. Each card is designed to be a quick-reference for sales conversations. Best when positioning is already established.

**Messaging Audit** (request with: "audit our messaging" or "review our current positioning"):
You will receive a diagnostic with specific calls on what is working, what is undifferentiated, what is missing, and rewritten alternatives. Provide the actual copy to be audited for the best results.
