## Target Segment

**Primary:** Series B through Series D SaaS companies (100-1,000 employees) with a customer success team of 5-30 people, experiencing customer churn concentrated in the first 90 days post-sale.

**Qualifying signals:**
- ARR between $10M and $150M
- Net revenue retention below 110%
- Time-to-value exceeding 30 days for most customers
- Customer success team spending 60%+ of time on manual onboarding tasks
- At least 50 new customer onboardings per quarter

**Out of scope (for now):** Enterprise (>1,000 employees) with custom procurement processes. SMB (<100 employees) with self-serve onboarding. Non-SaaS businesses.

## Positioning Statement

For Series B-C SaaS companies with first-90-day churn above 15%, Velora is the AI-powered onboarding platform that identifies at-risk customers within hours of signup, unlike CS platforms where onboarding is a feature, not the focus.

**Why this statement works:**
- The segment is specific (Series B-C, churn above 15%) — not all SaaS companies
- The differentiator is provable (identifies at-risk customers within hours)
- The competitive contrast is honest (CS platforms treat onboarding as a module)
- A competitor cannot honestly make this exact claim

## Layer 1: Strategic Narrative

### The Onboarding Crisis Nobody Talks About

SaaS companies have poured resources into acquiring customers. Marketing generates leads. Sales closes deals. And then the handoff happens — and everything gets quiet.

Customer onboarding is the highest-leverage, most neglected stage of the customer lifecycle. The data is unambiguous: 70% of customer churn is rooted in the first 90 days. Not because the product is wrong, but because the customer never reached the moment where value became obvious to them.

For a decade, the onboarding playbook has been the same: a CS rep, a spreadsheet, a series of check-in calls, and hope. This worked when companies onboarded 10 customers a quarter. It breaks at 50. It collapses at 200.

Meanwhile, every SaaS product is generating rich usage data — login frequency, feature adoption, workflow completion — that reveals exactly where customers get stuck. But this data sits in warehouses and dashboards, disconnected from the people running onboarding.

A new approach is emerging: onboarding that is intelligent by default. Systems that ingest product usage signals, identify at-risk customers before they disengage, and guide CS teams to the exact intervention that moves each customer forward. Not more check-in calls. Smarter ones.

Velora is built for this shift. We connect product usage data to onboarding workflows so that every customer gets a path to value — and every CS team knows exactly where to focus.

The companies that figure this out will not just reduce churn. They will turn onboarding into a growth engine — where activated customers expand, refer, and advocate.

## Layer 3: Key Message Pillars

### Pillar 1: Predict Who Gets Stuck — Before They Disengage

Velora's AI engine ingests product usage data in real time and compares each customer's onboarding trajectory against patterns from thousands of successful activations. When a customer deviates from the path to value, your team knows within hours — not weeks.

**Proof points:**
- Customers using predictive alerts intervene an average of 11 days earlier than those relying on scheduled check-ins.
- "We used to find out a customer was struggling when they missed their third call. Now Velora flags it the day after signup." — Director of CS, Clario (Series C, 400 employees)
- Velora's churn risk model achieves 84% accuracy in predicting which accounts will fail to activate within 90 days.

### Pillar 2: Replace Playbook Guesswork with Data-Driven Workflows

Traditional onboarding playbooks are static documents that assume every customer follows the same path. Velora builds dynamic workflows that adapt based on what each customer has actually done in the product — triggering the right content, task, or human touch at the right moment.

**Proof points:**
- Companies using Velora's adaptive workflows see 60% reduction in median time-to-value compared to their pre-Velora baseline.
- Velora integrates with 40+ product analytics, CRM, and communication tools — deployment takes an average of 4 days, not months.
- "Our onboarding used to be a 23-step checklist that every rep ran differently. Velora turned it into a system that runs the same way every time but adapts to each customer." — VP Customer Success, Stackline

### Pillar 3: Scale Onboarding Without Scaling Headcount

As your customer base grows, onboarding cannot depend on hiring more CS reps. Velora automates routine onboarding tasks — progress tracking, resource delivery, milestone nudges — so your team focuses human time on high-judgment interventions that actually require a person.

**Proof points:**
- Velora customers handle an average of 3.2x more concurrent onboardings per CS rep after implementation.
- Automated milestone tracking and resource delivery reduces manual CS tasks by 15 hours per rep per week.
- "We grew from 40 to 130 quarterly onboardings without adding a single CS hire. Velora is the reason." — Head of Customer Success, Procure.io

### Pillar 4: Turn Onboarding Data into Retention Intelligence

Velora does not just run onboarding — it measures it. Every onboarding generates structured data about what works: which content drives adoption, where customers stall, which interventions recover at-risk accounts. This intelligence feeds back into your playbooks, making onboarding systematically better over time.

**Proof points:**
- Velora's onboarding analytics dashboard shows activation rates, milestone completion, and intervention effectiveness by segment, cohort, and rep.
- Customers using Velora's feedback loop improve their 90-day activation rate by an average of 18 percentage points within two quarters.
- "For the first time, we can actually prove which parts of onboarding matter. We cut three steps that added zero value and doubled down on the two that correlated with retention." — CRO, DataLoom

## Competitive Response Guide

How to respond when a prospect mentions a specific alternative during the sales process.

### When they mention Gainsight or Totango:
"Great tools for ongoing customer success — health scoring, renewal management, expansion plays. The question is whether onboarding deserves a purpose-built solution or a module inside a broader platform. Our customers typically keep their CS platform for post-onboarding and use Velora for the first 90 days, where the data needs and workflow requirements are fundamentally different. We integrate natively with both Gainsight and Totango."

**Never say:** "Gainsight is too complex" or "Gainsight does not care about onboarding." Both are inaccurate and will undermine credibility.

### When they mention spreadsheets or manual processes:
"That approach works at a certain scale — and many of our customers started there. The inflection point is usually around 50 concurrent onboardings per quarter. At that point, the inconsistency between reps, the lack of visibility into which customers are stuck, and the inability to learn from past onboardings becomes a real constraint on growth. That is typically when teams come to us."

**Never say:** "Spreadsheets are terrible." The prospect built those spreadsheets. Respect their work while showing the ceiling.

### When they mention building in-house:
"Some of our best customers evaluated the build path first. The typical calculus is: 2-3 engineers for 4-6 months to build V1, then ongoing maintenance that competes with your product roadmap. And you still will not have the AI layer — the predictive models that learn from onboarding patterns across 120+ SaaS companies. Most teams conclude that engineering cycles are better spent on the core product."

**Never say:** "You cannot build this yourself." They can. The question is whether they should.

## Forbidden Language

Do not use these phrases in any external communication.

| Forbidden Phrase | Why | Use Instead |
|-----------------|-----|-------------|
| "Seamless onboarding" | Undifferentiated. Every onboarding tool claims this. Means nothing to buyers. | "Onboarding that adapts to each customer's product usage" |
| "All-in-one customer success platform" | We are not all-in-one. We are onboarding-focused. This also positions us against Gainsight where we lose. | "Purpose-built onboarding platform" |
| "Powered by AI" (as a headline) | AI is a method, not a benefit. Buyers are skeptical of AI claims without specifics. | "Predicts which customers will churn in the first 90 days" (the outcome of the AI) |
| "Easy to use" | Every product claims this. Unverifiable. Unspecific. | "Deploys in 4 days with no engineering resources required" |
| "Digital transformation" | Corporate jargon. No buyer wakes up wanting digital transformation. | "Replacing manual processes with intelligent automation" |
| "Best-in-class" | Superlative without evidence. Triggers skepticism. | Cite the specific metric: "84% prediction accuracy" or "60% reduction in time-to-value" |
| "Synergy" or "leverage" (as verbs) | Corporate speak that obscures meaning. | Use the plain English verb: "combines", "uses", "connects" |
| "Disruptive" | Self-congratulatory. Let analysts and customers call you disruptive. | Describe the shift you enable without labeling it |

## Positioning Traps We Avoided (and Why)

Documenting rejected positioning approaches is as important as documenting the chosen one. This section prevents future stakeholders from re-litigating decisions that were already evaluated.

### Rejected: "All-in-One Customer Success Platform"
**Why it was considered:** Broadening the category would increase TAM and allow us to compete with Gainsight directly.
**Why we rejected it:** We lose head-to-head against full CS platforms on feature breadth. Our strength is depth in onboarding. Broadening the category neutralizes our advantage and puts us in a fight we cannot win at our current stage. Revisit when product scope expands.

### Rejected: "The AI Company for Customer Success"
**Why it was considered:** AI is a strong differentiator and a market tailwind.
**Why we rejected it:** Leading with AI triggers skepticism without specifics. Buyers want to know what the AI does, not that it exists. We use AI as a proof point ("predicts churn with 84% accuracy") rather than the headline. The outcome is the message; AI is the method.

### Rejected: Positioning Against Gainsight Directly
**Why it was considered:** Gainsight is the most recognized name in CS. Positioning against them would clarify what we are.
**Why we rejected it:** Positioning by negation elevates the competitor. Most of our buyers are not evaluating Gainsight — they are evaluating the status quo (spreadsheets and manual processes). Positioning against Gainsight would be fighting the wrong battle. We mention Gainsight only in competitive response contexts, not in our primary positioning.

### Rejected: "Customer Onboarding Automation"
**Why it was considered:** Automation is a clear benefit and category descriptor.
**Why we rejected it:** "Automation" implies replacing humans with software. Our best customers value the combination of intelligent automation and human judgment. "AI-powered onboarding platform" better captures the augmentation angle. CS leaders do not want to automate away their teams — they want to make their teams more effective.
