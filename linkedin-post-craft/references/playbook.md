# LinkedIn Post Craft — Reference Playbook

Deeper/optional material for `linkedin-post-craft`: what to post when stuck, the milestone/journey
post shape with a full worked example, and turning a good run of posts into a durable asset. Pull
from this file when the situation calls for it — it doesn't need to be loaded for every post.

## Five prompts for when you don't know what to post

1. **React to a current trend or industry update** that affects your audience — "X just happened; here's what it means if you're in [role/industry]."
2. **Share something you learned recently** — this week, this quarter, in the last year. "I had no idea X, but this week I learned Y."
3. **Share something you learned "the hard way."** Mistakes and lessons are more relatable than polished wins.
4. **Share a milestone**, with real detail about why it matters, not just the announcement.
5. **Share something about your life outside work.** Humanizes the account; don't force it if it doesn't fit the audience/voice.

## Personal updates, journey posts, and milestones

A distinct shape from the educational/tip posts in the main skill — for sharing a number going up,
a launch anniversary, or any other progress marker. Five beats, in order:

1. **Share the update with real excitement** — lead with the number/result itself, stated
   energetically, not buried under context.
2. **Provide context** — introduce the journey/project briefly, for anyone encountering it for the
   first time.
3. **Include highlights that show progress** — concrete detail (a rule-of-three list works well
   here: subscriber count, a specific metric, a named win) so "progress" isn't just asserted.
4. **Show gratitude** — name the people/community involved specifically, not just "thanks
   everyone."
5. **(Optional) end with a passive CTA** — mention what's coming next, or that a link exists
   ("I'll drop the link in the comments"), without a hard ask. The update is the point of the
   post, not the ask.

**Worked example** (Devin Reed, newsletter milestone post):
> My newsletter just crossed 2k subscribers (!)
> I launched The Content Strategy Reeder to share how to create a cohesive, intentional, and
> effective content strategy. It's niche and focused by design.
> 6 months in, here's how it's goin'...
> — 2k+ double opt-in subscribers
> — 47% average open rate
> — Landed 1st sponsor, shout out to Alyce!
>
> Even cooler, the group is split 50/50 between sales and marketing pros — which shows the
> growing importance of having content creation skills, no matter your title.
>
> Sincere thank you to everyone who's subscribed (aka reeders!). I love reading your replies and
> seeing your wins on LinkedIn. I appreciate you saving a spot for me in your inbox.
>
> Next episode drops Saturday, where I'm breaking down a great example of LinkedIn personal
> branding and content strategy.
>
> PS: If you want to join, I'll put the link in the comments.

Mapped to the beats: excited number up top → one line of context on what the newsletter is →
three concrete highlights → a specific, named thank-you → a sneak peek of what's next plus a
passive link-in-comments close (never a hard "subscribe now").

**Why this works, structurally:** it entertains and informs the reader *first* — the actual
content value (the metrics, the insight about sales/marketing split) comes before anything that
serves the writer's own growth (the passive CTA at the very end). Reader value always outranks
self-promotion in the ordering, even in a post that's fundamentally about the writer's own
milestone.

## Turning a good run of posts into a durable asset

If a short run of related posts performs well (e.g. a week of daily tips), consider packaging them
into a single downloadable asset (a PDF, a one-pager) offered as a newsletter/email signup
incentive. This is a low-cost way to convert one-time post engagement into a durable, reusable
lead asset — worth flagging as an option when a post series does unusually well, not something to
plan every time.

**The reverse direction also works, and can be planned deliberately from the start** — most
content waterfalls start with one long piece chopped into micro-content, but going the other way
(build the long-form asset *out of* a planned run of posts) is just as valid:
1. Identify the actual growth goal (newsletter subscribers, Substack signups, contact-detail
   capture, etc.) — the asset's purpose should be decided before the posts are written, not
   bolted on after.
2. Choose a topic the audience genuinely cares about and that the writer can speak to credibly.
3. Post a series of genuinely useful, standalone tips on that topic — each one has to work as its
   own post, not read as a fragment of something else.
4. Package the tips into one asset (a lead magnet — checklist, one-pager, short guide).
5. Offer the asset in exchange for the specific growth action the goal in step 1 was actually
   after — contact details, a newsletter or Substack subscription, etc. Keep the exchange
   explicit and proportionate to the asset's value; this is the same call-to-value logic as
   `blog-writing-methodology`'s CTA/CTV guidance, applied to a lead-magnet exchange specifically.
