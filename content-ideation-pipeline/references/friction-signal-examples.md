# Friction-signal examples — good vs. bad extraction

Load this when you're unsure whether something counts as a friction signal worth pulling into
step 2 of the main triage, or whether a cluster (step 4) is grouped correctly.

## Good extraction: friction over features

**Bad (feature-request framing):**
> "The customer asked for a competitor comparison dashboard."

This tells you what the customer thinks the answer looks like. It's not wrong, but it's thin —
it gives you one content idea (a dashboard-shaped one) and closes off the rest.

**Good (friction framing, same underlying moment):**
> Speaker (VP Marketing, prospect call): "Honestly right now it's still a lot of guessing —
> we see a competitor ship something and we scramble to figure out if it actually moved
> anything, but we never really know until it's too late to react."

This is the same underlying situation, but pulled as friction it reveals the actual unmet need:
*not knowing if a competitor move mattered, in time to act on it*. That's broader than "a
dashboard" — it could become a pillar on early-warning signals, a supporting piece on how to
triage competitor releases, or a product-aware comparison piece, depending on what else clusters
with it. Extracting the feature request alone would have missed all of that.

## Good extraction: hedging language as a signal

> "I'm not sure how we'd even measure that, to be honest — I guess we just kind of eyeball it
> and hope."

The hedge ("I'm not sure," "I guess," "kind of") is doing the work here. It signals the speaker
doesn't have a confident method, not just that they lack a specific feature. That's a
problem-aware signal (see `output-taxonomy.md`) — there's no product vocabulary in it at all.

## Clustering: same gap, different words

These three quotes, pulled from three different sources, look unrelated on the surface:

1. (Sales call) "We just don't have a good read on whether we're ahead or behind."
2. (G2 review, "what do you dislike") "Hard to tell if changes we make are actually working."
3. (Support ticket) "Is there a way to see how we stack up over time, not just right now?"

Surface wording shares almost nothing. But all three point at the same underlying gap: **no
reliable way to measure directional progress over time.** That's a cluster with three
independent sources — a strong, recurring signal (see step 3, weighting) that could support a
pillar piece, not just a supporting one.

## What NOT to pull

- A quote that's purely a compliment or purely a complaint with no gap underneath it ("this is
  so slow," with no elaboration) — there's nothing to build content from until you know *why* it
  matters to them. Note it, but don't force it into a cluster.
- A one-off feature request with no friction language and no recurrence — usable as a minor
  data point, but don't let it anchor a cluster on its own. Flag it as anecdotal per step 3.
- Anything that only makes sense with a competitor named to explain it — pull it, but mark it for
  the compliance filter in step 7 before it goes anywhere near a public-facing angle.
