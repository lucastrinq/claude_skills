# Launch Communications Templates

Ready-to-adapt templates for internal and external launch communications. Replace bracketed placeholders with your specifics.

---

## Internal Communications

### Executive Summary (for Leadership)

**Subject: [Product/Feature] Launch — [Date] — Executive Summary**

**What:** We're launching [feature/product name], which [one sentence on what it does].

**Why now:** [2-3 sentences: customer demand signal, competitive pressure, strategic alignment]

**Key metrics we're targeting:**
- [Leading metric]: [target] by T+30
- [Lagging metric]: [target] by T+90
- Revenue impact: [estimate based on pipeline/win rate improvement]

**What we need from leadership:**
- [Specific ask — e.g., approval on pricing, exec quote for press release, customer introduction]

**Risk summary:** [One sentence on the biggest risk and mitigation]

**Timeline:** [Launch date]. Go/no-go decision on [date].

### All-Hands Talking Points

**For the presenter (CEO, VP Product, etc.) — not a script, a framework:**

**The problem:**
"[X% of our customers / N customers on the roadmap board] have been asking us for [capability]. Right now, they [describe the current workaround / pain]. That's not good enough."

**What we built:**
"[Feature name] lets [persona] do [specific thing]. [One concrete example of what it looks like in practice.]"

**Why it matters for us:**
"This is directly tied to our [Q/year] goal of [OKR or strategic initiative]. We expect this to [specific business impact — new revenue, reduced churn, competitive positioning]."

**What's next:**
"We launch on [date]. [What employees should know — if they'll see customer questions, if there's a demo they should watch, etc.]"

**Q&A prep (have answers ready for):**
- "Why did this take so long?"
- "What competitor already has this?"
- "What's the pricing impact?"
- "When does [related feature] ship?"

### Sales / CS Enablement Summary

**Subject: [Feature] Launch — What You Need to Know for Customer Conversations**

**What's launching:** [Feature name] — [one sentence description]

**Launch date:** [Date]

**Who cares:** [Primary persona] at [segment] companies, especially those who [specific trigger or pain point]

**Your talk track (30 seconds):**
"We just launched [feature], which means [buyer-centric benefit]. For your accounts, this is relevant because [connection to common pain point]. The biggest thing to know is [one key differentiator]."

**Top 3 things to mention in conversations:**
1. [Benefit + proof point]
2. [Benefit + proof point]
3. [Differentiator vs. competition]

**What NOT to say:**
- Don't promise [upcoming capability that isn't ready yet]
- Don't compare directly to [competitor] unless the customer brings it up first — instead [reframe approach]

**Objections you might hear:**
| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "[Common objection 1]" | [Talk track] |
| "[Common objection 2]" | [Talk track] |

**Resources:**
- Demo: [link to demo recording or environment]
- One-pager: [link]
- Help docs: [link]
- Questions? Slack #[channel] or ping [PMM name]

### Support Team FAQ

**Subject: [Feature] Launch — Support FAQ and Escalation Guide**

**What's launching:** [Feature name] — [one sentence]
**Launch date:** [Date]
**Expected ticket volume:** [Estimate — low/medium/high]

**Common questions and answers:**

**Q: How do I [most basic action]?**
A: [Step-by-step answer. Link to help doc.]

**Q: Does this work with [common integration/tool]?**
A: [Yes/No/Partially. Details. Link to docs.]

**Q: What's the pricing for this feature?**
A: [Included in X plan / Available as add-on / etc.]

**Q: I found a bug / something isn't working.**
A: [Triage steps. When to escalate. Who to escalate to.]

**Known issues at launch:**
- [Issue 1]: [Workaround]. Fix expected [date].
- [Issue 2]: [Workaround]. Fix expected [date].

**Escalation path:**
- Tier 1 (common questions): Handle with FAQ above
- Tier 2 (technical issues): Escalate to [team/person] via [channel]
- Tier 3 (critical bugs): Page [team] via [channel]. SLA: [time]

## External Communications

### Customer Announcement Email

**Segment: Power Users / Active Accounts**

Subject line options:
- [Feature name] is here — [one key benefit]
- You asked, we built it: [feature name]
- New: [Feature] — [benefit in 5 words or fewer]

Body:

Hi [Name],

[One sentence: the announcement.]

**What's new:** [Feature name] lets you [specific action in plain language]. [One sentence on the biggest benefit.]

**Why we built it:** [1-2 sentences connecting to customer feedback or pain point. Make them feel heard.]

**How to get started:** [1-2 steps. Link to feature or help doc.]

[Optional: one proof point from beta — "Beta customers saw X% improvement in Y."]

We'd love to hear what you think — reply to this email or share feedback at [link].

[Sign-off from a real person, not "The [Company] Team"]

---

**Segment: Low-Activity / At-Risk Accounts**

Subject line options:
- [Company] just got better for your team
- A reason to revisit [Company]: [feature name]

Body:

Hi [Name],

We've been making [Company] better, and I wanted to make sure you saw our latest update.

**[Feature name]** [one sentence on what it does — framed as solving a problem they might have experienced].

If [pain point they likely had] was part of why [Company] wasn't the right fit before, this might change that. [Link to see it in action.]

Happy to jump on a quick call if you'd like a walkthrough — just reply and I'll set it up.

[Personal sign-off]

### Blog Post Structure

**Title:** [Feature Name]: [Benefit-Driven Subtitle]

**Hook (first 2 sentences):** Start with the customer problem, not the feature announcement. "If you've ever [common frustration], you know [pain]. Today that changes."

**The announcement (1 paragraph):** What you're launching, in plain language. No jargon.

**Why we built it (1-2 paragraphs):** Customer demand, competitive context, strategic vision. Make the reader feel like this was inevitable.

**How it works (2-3 paragraphs + screenshots):** Walk through the key use cases. Show, don't just tell. Use real screenshots or GIFs.

**What makes it different (1 paragraph):** Your differentiator, briefly. Don't trash competitors — just state what's unique.

**What customers are saying (1-2 quotes):** Beta customer quotes with real names if possible.

**What's next (1 paragraph):** Briefly mention what's coming. Builds confidence in ongoing investment.

**CTA:** "Try it now" / "Learn more" / "Get started"
