## Annotations — Why This Works

### Header: "Async Standups That Actually Work"
- **Why it works:** Immediately signals the category (async standups) and differentiates from failed attempts ("actually work" — acknowledges skepticism). Seven words. Memorable.
- **Common mistake:** "AI-Powered Collaborative Team Intelligence Platform" — jargon that means nothing to the reader.

### Problem Statement: "5+ hours per week in status meetings"
- **Why it works:** Leads with a specific, quantified pain the reader recognizes. Uses "your" to make it personal. No product mentioned yet — earns the right to pitch by proving you understand the problem.
- **Common mistake:** "Trelliswork is an innovative async communication platform..." — nobody cares what you are until they believe you understand their problem.

### Solution Overview: One Sentence
- **Why it works:** Plain language. Says what it does (replaces standups), how (async check-ins), and the payoff (alignment without disrupting flow). Reader knows in 10 seconds whether to keep reading.
- **Common mistake:** Two paragraphs about your founding story or mission statement.

### Key Capabilities: 4 Bullets
- **Why it works:** Each bullet is **Feature → Benefit** format. Bold the feature, explain why the buyer cares. "AI summarizes" isn't the point — "less typing for engineers" is.
- **Common mistake:** 8-12 feature bullets with no benefit translation. "Real-time WebSocket architecture" — the reader is a VP Engineering, not evaluating your tech stack in a one-pager.

### Proof Points: Real Numbers
- **Why it works:** Named company (Vercel), specific metric (4 hours/week), specific scope (160 hours/month). The reader can validate this claim. Also includes a benchmark study with sample size (n=340). Numbers build trust.
- **Common mistake:** "Trusted by leading companies" with unnamed logos. Or "customers love us" with no evidence.

### Differentiation: Named Alternatives
- **Why it works:** Addresses the three things the reader is already comparing against: Slack (free/DIY), existing bots (Geekbot), and status quo (keeping meetings). Specific claims, not vague superiority.
- **Common mistake:** "Unlike the competition, we're better." Nobody believes this. Or worse — ignoring competition entirely as if the buyer has no alternatives.

### CTA: One Action
- **Why it works:** Two options that match different intent levels (free trial for self-serve, call for enterprise). No "learn more" — that's not an action.
- **Common mistake:** Five CTAs (read our blog, join our community, follow us on Twitter, download the whitepaper, AND book a demo). Paradox of choice = no action.

## Layout Notes (For Design Handoff)

- **Target: 1 printed page** when formatted. If it doesn't fit on one page, cut content — don't shrink fonts.
- **Visual hierarchy:** Company logo top-left. Tagline as largest text. Capabilities as bullet list with bold leads. Proof points in a highlighted callout box. CTA at bottom with contrasting color.
- **White space:** Don't fill every pixel. Scannable > comprehensive.
- **Mobile consideration:** Sales reps share these from their phones. Test how it looks on a 6" screen.
