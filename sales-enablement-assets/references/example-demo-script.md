# Example: Demo Script

**Product:** InsightFlow — Customer Feedback Analytics Platform
**Target persona:** VP Customer Experience, Mid-Market SaaS (500-2000 employees)
**Demo length:** 25 minutes total
**Pre-requisite:** Discovery call completed. Prospect's top priorities: reducing time spent manually tagging feedback, identifying churn signals earlier, and getting executive-ready reports.

---

## Opening (2 minutes)

**[SHOW: Title slide with prospect's company name + agenda]**

> "Thanks for making the time, [Name]. Based on our last conversation, I've put together a demo focused on three things you told me matter most: cutting down the manual work of tagging and categorizing feedback, catching churn signals before they become cancellations, and getting your leadership team the reports they need without spending half your week building them.

> I'll walk through each of those with real scenarios. Stop me anytime — if something doesn't match your world, I'd rather know now so I can adjust."

**Timing note:** Don't rush this. Confirming priorities builds trust and lets you pivot if something's changed since discovery.

## Discovery Recap (1 minute)

**[SHOW: Prospect's stated priorities — a simple bullet slide or verbal recap]**

> "Quick recap to make sure I have this right. You told me your team is spending roughly 15 hours a week manually categorizing NPS and support ticket feedback. You've had two surprise churns this quarter where the warning signs were in the data but nobody connected the dots. And your CEO wants a monthly CX dashboard but right now that takes you two days to put together. Is that still the picture?"

**[PAUSE — wait for confirmation or correction.]**

**If they add something new:** "Good to know. Let me make sure I address that too."

**If priorities shifted:** Adjust the demo order. Lead with whatever they now care about most.

## Demo Narrative — Use Case 1: Automated Feedback Categorization (6-7 minutes)

**[SHOW: Dashboard with incoming feedback stream — NPS, support tickets, app reviews, social mentions]**

> "So here's where everything lands. InsightFlow is already pulling in feedback from [their actual tools if known — e.g., Intercom, Zendesk, App Store]. In your case, you mentioned you're on Zendesk and Intercom — both are native integrations, no engineering work required."

**[SHOW: AI-categorized feedback with auto-tags]**

> "Every piece of feedback gets categorized automatically. Not just sentiment — positive, negative, neutral — but topic, product area, and customer segment. So this NPS response saying 'I love the product but the reporting is painful' gets tagged as: sentiment = mixed, topic = reporting, product area = analytics, and because we know this customer is on the Enterprise plan, it's also tagged by segment."

**[PAUSE]** "How does that compare to how your team does it today?"

**[Listen. They'll likely describe the manual process. Empathize, then continue.]**

**[SHOW: Category accuracy metrics]**

> "We see 92% accuracy out of the box, and it gets better as you validate — you can approve or correct any tag, and the model learns from your corrections. Most teams get to 97%+ within the first month."

**Aha moment setup:**

> "Here's the part I think matters for your team specifically. Watch what happens when I filter by 'Enterprise' and 'Negative' and 'Last 30 days'..."

**[SHOW: Filtered view revealing a clear pattern — e.g., 14 enterprise customers complaining about the same feature]**

> "Fourteen enterprise customers flagged the same issue in the last month. That's the kind of pattern that takes weeks to spot manually — or never gets spotted because the feedback is split across Zendesk, NPS surveys, and app reviews. InsightFlow surfaces it in seconds because all the feedback is in one place, categorized consistently."

**Transition:** "That's the categorization side. Now let me show you what happens when we start connecting feedback to churn risk."

## Demo Narrative — Use Case 2: Churn Signal Detection (5-6 minutes)

**[SHOW: Churn risk dashboard with customer health indicators]**

> "You mentioned two surprise churns this quarter. The worst part about surprise churn is that it's rarely actually a surprise — the signals were there, they just weren't connected. That's what this view does."

**[SHOW: A specific customer card with escalating negative signals over time]**

> "Here's what a churn trajectory looks like. This customer — let's call them 'Acme Corp' — sent a support ticket in January that was mildly negative. February, they submitted an NPS of 6 with a comment about missing features. March, their usage dropped 40%. Each signal individually seems manageable. Together, they're a churn trajectory."

**[SHOW: Alert that was triggered]**

> "InsightFlow flagged Acme as 'at-risk' in mid-February — before the usage drop, based on the sentiment trend alone. Your CS team would have gotten this alert, with the full context: 'This Enterprise customer's sentiment has declined across 3 feedback channels in 6 weeks. Key themes: missing reporting features, slow support response.'"

**[PAUSE]** "Would that kind of early warning have changed the outcome for the two accounts you lost?"

**If they ask "How early does it catch signals?"**
> "It depends on feedback volume. For customers who interact with you regularly — support tickets, surveys, app usage — we typically flag risk 4-6 weeks before a churn event. For customers with less feedback data, we supplement with usage pattern analysis."

**If they ask "What about false positives?"**
> "You can tune the sensitivity. Most teams start with medium sensitivity and adjust after the first month. We'd rather over-flag than miss a real churn signal — you can always dismiss an alert, but you can't undo a lost customer."

**Transition:** "Last thing — let me show you how all of this becomes the executive dashboard your CEO is asking for."

## Demo Narrative — Use Case 3: Executive Reporting (4-5 minutes)

**[SHOW: Executive dashboard — clean, visual, high-level]**

> "This is what your CEO would see. No raw data, no spreadsheets — just the metrics that matter. Customer sentiment trend over time, top emerging themes, churn risk distribution, and the feedback-to-action pipeline."

**[SHOW: One-click report generation]**

> "You mentioned spending two days building the monthly CX report. Watch this."

**[CLICK: Generate monthly report]**

> "That's your monthly report. Auto-generated, pulling from all feedback sources, with the top themes, trend analysis, and recommended actions. You can customize the template once and it regenerates every month — or weekly, or whatever cadence you want."

**[SHOW: Report output — polished, board-ready]**

> "You can export it as PDF, send it as a scheduled email, or share it as a live dashboard link. Your CEO always has the latest data without you having to lift a finger."

**[PAUSE]** "If this existed today, what would you do with the two days you'd get back each month?"

**[Listen. This answer reveals their real pain and their vision — use it in the close.]**

## Proof (3 minutes)

**[SHOW: Customer story slide — or tell it verbally]**

> "Let me share a quick story that might resonate. [Customer Name], a SaaS company about your size — 800 employees, 3-person CX team — was in a similar spot. They were spending 20+ hours a week manually analyzing feedback and still missing churn signals.

> After implementing InsightFlow, they reduced manual feedback analysis by 85% — from 20 hours to about 3 hours a week. They caught their first at-risk customer that would have churned within the first month. And their VP of CX told me: 'For the first time, I'm presenting insights to the executive team instead of data. The conversation has completely changed.'

> Within 6 months, they attributed $180K in saved revenue directly to early churn intervention."

## Close (5 minutes)

> "[Name], let me summarize what we covered. You told me the three things that matter most are reducing manual categorization work, catching churn signals earlier, and getting executive-ready reports.

> Based on what you've seen today — does this feel like it addresses those three priorities?"

**[PAUSE — get their reaction. Address any concerns that surface.]**

> "What would be the most useful next step? I'd suggest a technical evaluation where we connect your actual Zendesk and Intercom data and you see your real feedback categorized by InsightFlow. That usually takes about a day to set up. Would next week work to kick that off?"

**If they need stakeholder buy-in:**
> "Totally understand. Would it help if I put together a one-pager you can share with [stakeholder name/role]? I can tailor it to what they'd care about most."

**If they want pricing:**
> "Happy to walk through pricing. For a team your size, we're typically in the [range] — but let me put together a proposal based on your specific volume after the technical evaluation, so the numbers are precise."

## Contingency Branches

**If they ask about data security/compliance:**
> "Great question. We're SOC2 Type II certified, and all data is encrypted in transit and at rest. We never train our models on your customer data. I can send you our security whitepaper after this call — would that be helpful?"

**If they ask about competitor X:**
> Don't trash them. "They're a solid product. Where we tend to differentiate is [specific differentiator relevant to their priorities]. But honestly, the best way to compare is to see both products with your actual data."

**If the demo breaks / something doesn't load:**
> "Looks like that's taking a moment. While it loads — let me tell you what you'd see here..." [Describe the feature verbally, then move on. Never wait awkwardly. Never apologize more than once.]
