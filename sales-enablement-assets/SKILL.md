# Sales Enablement Asset Creator

Produce content that sales reps actually use in live deals — not marketing collateral that collects dust in a shared drive. Every asset is structured for the way reps actually work: scannable, customizable, buyer-centric, and tied to a specific deal stage and persona.

## Workflow

### Phase 1: Need Clarification

**Route to the right asset type.** Don't start writing until you know exactly what's needed.

Ask these questions:
1. **What deal stage?** (Awareness → Consideration → Decision → Expansion)
2. **What persona?** (Economic buyer, technical buyer, end user, champion)
3. **What context?** (New opportunity, competitive deal, expansion, objection handling)
4. **What format?** (One-pager, deck, script, calculator, guide)

If the user isn't sure, use the Asset Router table below to recommend.

**Quality gate:** Do not start creating content until you have deal stage + persona + format confirmed.

### Phase 2: Input Gathering

**Minimum viable inputs (must have at least these):**
- Product name and what it does
- Target buyer persona and their primary pain
- Key differentiator (what makes you different from alternatives)

**Ideal inputs (produce significantly better output):**
- Existing messaging/positioning framework
- Customer proof points (quotes, metrics, case studies)
- Competitive context (who else is in the deal)
- Specific objections the rep is hearing
- Pricing/packaging information
- Company/brand voice guidelines

If ideal inputs aren't available, work with what you have and flag where the asset would be stronger with additional inputs.

### Phase 3: Asset Creation

**Read the relevant example file before writing:**
- For one-pagers: read [references/example-one-pager.md](references/example-one-pager.md)
- For demo scripts: read [references/example-demo-script.md](references/example-demo-script.md)

Follow the appropriate template from the Asset Templates section below. Write to these standards:

**Voice rules:**
- Write how a confident rep would actually speak — not how marketing would write a press release
- Lead with the buyer's problem, not your product's features
- Use "you/your" more than "we/our"
- Specific beats vague: "reduce onboarding time from 6 weeks to 5 days" beats "accelerate onboarding"
- Every claim needs a proof point or gets marked `[ADD PROOF POINT]`

**Structure rules:**
- One idea per section
- Scannable in 30 seconds (bullets over paragraphs, bold key phrases)
- Every section answers "why should the buyer care?"
- End every asset with a clear, specific next step

### Phase 4: Sales Usability Review

**Read [references/asset-quality-checklist.md](references/asset-quality-checklist.md) and score the asset.**

Apply these pass/fail checks:
1. **The 30-second test:** Can a rep scan this and find what they need in 30 seconds?
2. **The customization test:** Can a rep personalize this for a specific deal in under 5 minutes?
3. **The buyer test:** If a prospect saw this, would they feel understood or marketed-at?
4. **The proof test:** Is every claim backed by evidence, or is it just assertions?
5. **The action test:** Is the next step crystal clear?

If any check fails, revise before delivery.

### Phase 5: Variant Generation

If the user needs persona or segment variants:
1. Start with the strongest version (usually the primary buyer persona)
2. Create variants by adjusting: pain points, proof points, language level, and CTA
3. Keep the core structure identical — only change persona-specific content
4. Label each variant clearly: "[Product] One-Pager — VP Engineering" vs. "— CTO"

## Asset Router

| Deal Stage | Buyer Need | Recommended Asset | Typical Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Awareness** | Educate on the problem | Problem overview / thought leadership piece | 1-2 pages |
| **Awareness** | Introduce your approach | Elevator pitch / company overview one-pager | 1 page |
| **Consideration** | Evaluate your solution | Solution one-pager | 1 page |
| **Consideration** | See the product in action | Demo script with narrative flow | 3-5 pages (internal) |
| **Consideration** | Compare to alternatives | Competitive comparison / battlecard summary | 1-2 pages |
| **Decision** | Justify the investment | ROI / business case calculator | 1-2 pages + model |
| **Decision** | Validate with proof | Case study / customer story | 1-2 pages |
| **Decision** | Address procurement concerns | Security overview / compliance summary | 1-2 pages |
| **Decision** | Understand implementation | Implementation overview / onboarding guide | 2-3 pages |
| **Expansion** | Drive additional adoption | New feature brief / upsell one-pager | 1 page |
| **Expansion** | Renew or expand contract | Success recap / value realization report | 2-3 pages |

## Asset Templates

### Solution One-Pager

The single most-used sales asset. Gets emailed after first calls, shared in Slack, and left behind in meetings.

**Structure (aim for 1 page when formatted):**

1. **Header**: Product name + tagline (7 words or fewer)
2. **Problem statement** (2-3 sentences): The pain in the buyer's words. No product mentions yet.
3. **Solution overview** (2-3 sentences): What you do, in plain language. How you solve the problem above.
4. **Key capabilities** (3-4 bullets): Feature → benefit format. "**[Feature]** — [What it means for the buyer]"
5. **Proof points** (2-3): Customer metrics, quotes, or third-party validation. Real numbers only.
6. **Why us** (2-3 bullets): Differentiation. What makes you different from the way they'd solve this without you.
7. **CTA**: One clear next step with contact path.

### Demo Script

Not a feature tour. A narrative that connects the buyer's problems to your product through their use cases.

**Structure:**

1. **Opening** (2 min): Confirm agenda, state what you'll show and why it matters to them
2. **Discovery recap** (1 min): "Last time we spoke, you mentioned [pain]. Is that still the top priority?"
3. **Demo narrative** (15-20 min): Organized by 2-3 buyer use cases, NOT by product features
   - For each use case: pain reminder → show the workflow → pause for reaction → connect to outcome
   - Include "aha moment" — the single most impressive thing. Build to it, don't lead with it.
   - Include pause points: "How does that compare to what you do today?"
4. **Proof** (3 min): One relevant customer story with a specific metric
5. **Close** (5 min): Summarize what resonated, address concerns, propose next step

### ROI / Business Case

**Components:**
1. **Current state costs**: Labor hours, tool costs, opportunity costs (quantified)
2. **Future state with your solution**: Time saved, efficiency gained, revenue enabled
3. **Investment required**: License + implementation + internal time (be honest)
4. **Payback period**: When do they break even?
5. **3-year value**: Total benefit minus total cost
6. **Sensitivity analysis**: Conservative / moderate / aggressive scenarios

### Case Study / Customer Story

**Structure (Situation → Challenge → Solution → Results):**
1. **Customer snapshot**: Industry, size, relevant context (1-2 sentences)
2. **Challenge**: The specific problem, quantified if possible
3. **Why they chose you**: Decision criteria and what tipped them (2-3 sentences)
4. **Solution**: What they implemented, how long it took (2-3 sentences)
5. **Results**: 2-3 specific metrics with timeframes. "X% improvement in Y within Z months."
6. **Quote**: One sentence from the champion that captures the transformation

### Objection Handling Guide

**For each objection (aim for top 6-8):**

| Objection | What They're Really Asking | Response Framework | Proof Point |
|---|---|---|---|
| "[Exact words]" | [The underlying concern] | Acknowledge → Reframe → Evidence → Bridge to value | [Specific customer data or quote] |

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It's Bad | Do This Instead |
|---|---|---|
| Feature dumps | Listing every feature overwhelms buyers and sounds like every other vendor. Reps can't use it in conversation. | Pick the 3-4 capabilities that matter to THIS persona. Connect each to a buyer outcome. |
| Marketing voice in sales content | Reps won't read "leverage our best-in-class platform." It sounds fake and they can't say it out loud. | Write how a confident human talks. Short sentences. Concrete examples. No jargon. |
| Too long | If it's more than 1 page for a one-pager or 12 slides for a deck, nobody reads it. Sales content competes with 100 other things for attention. | Ruthlessly cut. If a section doesn't change the buyer's behavior, delete it. |
| No proof points | Claims without evidence are just marketing assertions. Buyers are skeptical and reps feel exposed. | Every claim needs a number, a customer quote, or a third-party validation. Flag gaps with `[ADD PROOF POINT]`. |
| Not customizable | Generic content that can't be tailored to a specific deal gets used once and abandoned. | Build in clear customization points. Use brackets for personalization: "[Customer name]'s specific situation." |
| Missing the CTA | Beautiful content with no clear next step wastes the moment. The buyer reads it and then... nothing. | Every asset ends with ONE specific action. Not "learn more" — "Schedule a 30-minute technical review with [name]." |
| Building for the wrong stage | Sending a product overview to someone ready to buy (or an ROI calculator to someone who doesn't know they have a problem) kills deals. | Match the asset to the deal stage. Use the Asset Router. When in doubt, ask the rep what conversation they need to have. |
