# Competitive Intelligence Framework

## Purpose

Competitive intelligence is not about knowing everything about competitors — it's about knowing the right things to win the right deals. A useful CI system answers three questions: Where are we vulnerable? Where are we strongest? Where is the market heading that neither of us has addressed yet?

---

## Part 1: Intel Gathering

### Primary Sources (highest signal)

| Source | What to extract | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| **Win/loss interviews** | Why deals go to competitors in buyers' own words | Per deal, or monthly batch |
| **Sales call recordings** | Objections raised, competitor names mentioned, pricing reactions | Weekly review |
| **Customer advisory boards** | How customers compare you to alternatives they've evaluated | Quarterly |
| **Churned customer interviews** | Why they left and what they chose instead | Per churn event |

### Secondary Sources (directional signal)

| Source | What to extract |
|---|---|
| **Competitor websites / pricing pages** | Positioning shifts, new features, ICP changes, pricing model |
| **G2 / Capterra / Trustpilot reviews** | Unfiltered complaints = their real weaknesses; praise = their genuine strengths |
| **Job postings** | What they're building next (hiring for) and where they're investing |
| **Press releases / funding announcements** | Strategic direction, new segments, acquisition signals |
| **LinkedIn / social** | Messaging pivots, new campaigns, exec commentary |
| **SEO / ad tools (Semrush, SpyFu)** | Keywords they're buying = segments they're targeting |

### Intel Capture Template (per competitor, per quarter)

```
Competitor: [Name]
Date: [Quarter]
Source(s): [Where this came from]

What changed this quarter:
- Positioning: [Any shifts in how they describe themselves]
- Product: [New features, integrations, removals]
- Pricing: [Changes in model, tiers, discounting behavior]
- ICP: [New segments or personas they're targeting]
- GTM: [New channels, partnerships, campaigns]

Win/loss impact:
- Deals we won against them: [#] — primary reason: [X]
- Deals we lost to them: [#] — primary reason: [X]
- Net competitive trend: ↑ Gaining / → Stable / ↓ Losing ground
```

## Part 2: Competitor Profile

Build one profile per direct competitor. Refresh quarterly or after any major move.

**[Competitor Name]**

| Dimension | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Founded / funding** | [Year, total raised, last round + date] |
| **Estimated scale** | [ARR estimate, headcount, customer count if known] |
| **Target ICP** | [Company size, industry, persona] |
| **Core positioning** | [Their headline claim — verbatim from their website] |
| **Top 3 strengths** | [What they genuinely do well — be honest] |
| **Top 3 weaknesses** | [Real gaps, not wishful thinking — confirmed by buyer feedback] |
| **Pricing model** | [€/user/month, flat fee, enterprise contract, freemium?] |
| **Key integrations** | [What tech stack do they plug into?] |
| **Strategic direction** | [Where are they investing based on hiring + product signals?] |
| **Threat level** | High / Medium / Low — [1-sentence justification] |

## Part 3: Competitive Positioning Map

Choose two axes that buyers actually use to evaluate options (not axes that make you look good). See `consultant-frameworks/references/competitive-landscape.md` for axis selection guidance.

Plot each competitor. Identify:
- **Your quadrant**: Are you differentiated or in a crowded space?
- **White space**: High-demand area with no strong incumbent
- **Threat zone**: Competitors moving toward your position

## Part 4: Battlecard Template

One battlecard per direct competitor. Used by Sales in live deals.

---
**Competing against: [Competitor Name]**
*Last updated: [Date] | Owner: [PMM name]*

**When you're in a deal against them, lead with:**
> "Unlike [Competitor], which [their approach/limitation], we [your differentiator] — which means you [customer outcome]."

**We typically win when:**
- [Situation 1 — be specific: deal size, use case, persona]
- [Situation 2]
- [Situation 3]

**We typically lose when:**
- [Situation 1 — be honest]
- [Situation 2]

**Their top 3 traps (what they say vs. reality):**
1. They claim: "[Quote from their website/pitch]" → Reality: [Honest reframe]
2. They claim: "[...]" → Reality: [...]
3. They claim: "[...]" → Reality: [...]

**Proof points to use against them:**
- [Customer quote specific to this competitive situation]
- [Metric that demonstrates differentiated value vs. this competitor]
- [Case study where you displaced them or beat them in evaluation]

**Pricing guidance:**
- Their typical deal: [€X range, typical discount behavior]
- Our response: [When to hold, when to flex, what to bundle]

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Monitoring everything, acting on nothing | Assign a quarterly CI owner; produce 1 actionable output per cycle |
| Letting Sales write their own competitive positioning | PMM owns battlecards; Sales inputs win/loss signal |
| Describing competitor weaknesses without proof | Every weakness claim must be backed by buyer quotes or review data |
| Ignoring "do nothing" as a competitor | Status quo (spreadsheets, manual process, no solution) is often your real competitor |
| Treating CI as a one-time project | Set a quarterly cadence; competitive landscape drifts fast |
