# Win/Loss Analysis

## Purpose

Win/loss analysis answers the question competitors and internal assumptions never can: **why do buyers actually choose us — or not?** Done well, it's the highest-signal input for messaging, ICP refinement, product roadmap, and sales enablement.

The goal is not to collect opinions. It's to find **patterns** that can change decisions.

---

## Part 1: Interview Design

### Who to Interview

| Segment | Why | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| **Recent losses** | Freshest signal; buyers will say what Sales won't report | Within 2 weeks of closed-lost |
| **Recent wins** | Reveals what actually drove the decision (vs. what Sales thinks) | Within 2 weeks of closed-won |
| **Churned customers** | Post-purchase regret = product-market fit gaps | Within 30 days of churn |
| **Competitive displacements** | Why they switched away from a competitor to you | Within 4 weeks of close |

Target: **minimum 2 interviews per segment per quarter**. Patterns emerge at ~8–10 interviews.

### Who Conducts the Interview

Never Sales. Win/loss interviews must be conducted by PMM, Customer Success, or a neutral third party. Buyers won't tell Sales the real reason they lost — they'll be polite. They'll tell a neutral party the truth.

### Interview Guide (30 minutes)

**Opening (2 min)**
> "Thanks for your time. I'm [Name] from [Company] — I'm on the product marketing team, not in sales. This is a research conversation, not a sales call. Everything you share is confidential and used to improve how we work. There are no wrong answers."

**The evaluation process (8 min)**
1. "Can you walk me through what triggered the search for a solution?"
2. "Who was involved in the evaluation — who had input, who had final say?"
3. "What were the top 3 criteria you used to evaluate options?"
4. "Which solutions did you evaluate seriously?"

**The decision (10 min)**
5. "What ultimately drove the final decision?"
6. *(If loss)* "What did [chosen vendor] have that tipped it for you?"
7. *(If win)* "Was there a moment where you felt confident we were the right choice?"
8. "Was there anything about our product, process, or team that gave you pause?"

**Pricing and process (5 min)**
9. "How did pricing factor in?"
10. "Was there anything in our sales process that could have been better?"

**Forward-looking (5 min)**
11. "If we made one change to be a stronger fit for companies like yours, what would it be?"
12. "Is there anything I didn't ask that you think we should know?"

## Part 2: Pattern Analysis

Individual interviews = anecdotes. Patterns across interviews = actionable intelligence.

After every 5–10 interviews, run a pattern analysis:

### Frequency Matrix

| Theme | Win mentions | Loss mentions | Net signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ease of implementation | 7 | 1 | Strong win driver |
| Pricing / value perception | 2 | 6 | Loss driver — investigate |
| Integration with [Tool X] | 5 | 2 | Win driver |
| Enterprise security features | 1 | 5 | Loss driver — product gap |
| Sales responsiveness | 6 | 1 | Win driver |
| [Theme N] | | | |

Themes appearing in **3+ losses** with no corresponding wins = critical gaps. Escalate to product or pricing immediately.

### Decision Driver Taxonomy

Classify every stated reason into one of four buckets (MECE):

| Bucket | Definition | Action owner |
|---|---|---|
| **Product gap** | We don't have a feature/capability they needed | Product |
| **Positioning gap** | We have it but they didn't know / didn't believe it | PMM |
| **Process gap** | Sales or CS failed in execution | Sales Enablement / CS |
| **Fit gap** | We're genuinely not the right solution for this buyer | ICP refinement |

This taxonomy prevents the common failure of treating every loss as a product problem or every loss as a sales problem.

## Part 3: Win/Loss Output Formats

### Quarterly Win/Loss Report (for leadership)

**Executive summary** (SCR structure):
- Situation: [# deals analyzed, time period, win rate]
- Complication: [Top loss driver and its frequency]
- Resolution: [Top 2 recommended actions with owners]

**Win drivers** (top 3, with frequency and representative quote)
**Loss drivers** (top 3, with frequency, bucket classification, and recommended action)
**ICP signal** (are we winning in the right accounts? Losing in the wrong ones?)
**Competitive signal** (which competitors appeared most, and in which context?)
**One chart**: Win rate by deal size / segment / source — to show where the pattern is concentrated

### Sales Enablement Output (from win/loss)

After each report cycle, produce:
- Updated battlecard(s) if competitive patterns shifted
- New objection-handling talk track for the top loss driver
- ICP refinement note if a clear out-of-profile pattern emerged

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Having Sales run win/loss interviews | Use PMM or a neutral party — buyers are polite to Sales |
| Treating every loss as a product problem | Use the decision driver taxonomy to correctly classify |
| Only interviewing losses | Win interviews reveal what you're doing right AND confirm ICP |
| Reporting themes without recommendations | Every pattern must have an owner and an action |
| Annual cadence | Quarterly minimum; monthly if deal volume allows |
| Confirmation bias in analysis | Let the buyer's exact words lead — don't retrofit to internal narratives |
