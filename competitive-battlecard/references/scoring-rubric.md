# Battlecard Scoring Rubric

Score each dimension 1-5. Total possible: 50. Apply thresholds at the bottom.

## Dimensions

### 1. Evidence Density

Every competitive claim should have a proof point (customer quote, data, third-party source, or verifiable fact).

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 5 | 90%+ of claims have specific, verifiable proof points with sources |
| 4 | 75-89% of claims have proof points; remaining are flagged `[VERIFY]` |
| 3 | 50-74% of claims have proof points; some unsupported assertions |
| 2 | Under 50% of claims backed by evidence; mostly assertions |
| 1 | Almost no evidence — reads like opinion, not intelligence |

### 2. Actionability

Can a rep actually use this in a live deal? Are talk tracks conversational? Are trap questions usable?

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 5 | Every section gives the rep something to say or do. Talk tracks sound natural. A rep could read this 5 minutes before a call and feel prepared. |
| 4 | Most sections are actionable. Talk tracks are good but a few read like press releases. |
| 3 | Mix of actionable and theoretical content. Some sections are "nice to know" but not usable in a call. |
| 2 | Mostly informational. Rep would need to translate this into their own talk tracks. |
| 1 | Academic analysis. Interesting but a rep couldn't use it without significant rework. |

### 3. Specificity

Are claims and comparisons specific, or vague generalizations?

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 5 | Comparisons cite specific features, metrics, or customer outcomes. "Their API latency averages 340ms vs. our 45ms" not "we're faster." |
| 4 | Most comparisons are specific. A few resort to vague terms like "more robust" or "better experience." |
| 3 | Mix of specific and vague. Several claims could apply to any competitor. |
| 2 | Mostly vague. "Better," "faster," "more intuitive" without specifics. |
| 1 | Entirely generic. Could swap in any competitor's name and the content would be unchanged. |

### 4. Competitive Honesty

Does the battlecard honestly acknowledge competitor strengths?

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 5 | "Where They Win" section is honest and specific. Includes neutralization strategies that acknowledge the gap rather than denying it. |
| 4 | Acknowledges main competitor strengths. Neutralization strategies are mostly credible. |
| 3 | Mentions competitor strengths but minimizes them. Neutralization strategies lean toward spin. |
| 2 | "Where They Win" section is superficial or backhanded. "They have a nice UI but no real depth." |
| 1 | No honest acknowledgment of competitor strengths. Pure propaganda — reps won't trust it. |

### 5. Trap Question Quality

Are trap questions genuine discovery questions that happen to expose competitor weaknesses?

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 5 | Every trap question sounds like a natural, curious discovery question. A prospect would never feel interrogated. Each includes a clear note on what the answer reveals. |
| 4 | Most questions are natural. One or two might feel slightly leading if a prospect thinks about it. |
| 3 | Mix of natural and somewhat obvious questions. "Have you noticed [competitor] struggles with X?" |
| 2 | Most questions transparently target competitor weaknesses. Prospects would see through them. |
| 1 | Questions are adversarial attacks disguised as questions. Would damage trust with the prospect. |

### 6. Objection Handling Depth

Are objection responses useful talk tracks or generic dismissals?

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 5 | Each objection has: the exact words a prospect uses, the underlying concern, a conversational response framework, and a specific proof point. Responses acknowledge the objection before reframing. |
| 4 | Good coverage of top objections. Responses are mostly conversational with proof points. |
| 3 | Common objections covered but responses are somewhat scripted or miss the underlying concern. |
| 2 | Objections listed with surface-level responses. "Our product is actually great at that." |
| 1 | Objection handling is dismissive or missing entirely. |

### 7. Freshness & Accuracy

Is the intelligence current? Are claims accurate based on available information?

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 5 | All intel is dated. Pricing, features, and positioning reflect the competitor's current state. Any stale intel is flagged. |
| 4 | Mostly current. A few data points may be 6+ months old but are noted. |
| 3 | Mix of current and potentially outdated intel. No dates on claims. |
| 2 | Several claims feel outdated. Competitor may have addressed cited weaknesses. |
| 1 | Intel is clearly stale. References outdated pricing, deprecated features, or old positioning. |

### 8. Scannability

Can a rep find what they need in 30 seconds during a live call?

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 5 | Clear section headers, bullet points, bold key phrases. Quick reference table at top. A rep could find the right objection response mid-call. |
| 4 | Well-structured with clear sections. Most content is scannable. A few dense paragraphs. |
| 3 | Sections exist but some are wall-of-text. Would take a minute+ to find specific info. |
| 2 | Poorly organized. Long paragraphs with buried insights. |
| 1 | Unstructured wall of text. Unusable in a live deal situation. |

### 9. Positioning Clarity

Is there a clear, memorable narrative frame that a rep can internalize?

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 5 | The positioning narrative is 2-3 sentences, memorable, and passes the "bar test" — a rep could explain it to a friend. It frames every section that follows. |
| 4 | Clear positioning narrative. Mostly memorable. Connects to the rest of the battlecard. |
| 3 | Positioning exists but is generic or too long to remember. Doesn't clearly frame the battlecard. |
| 2 | No clear positioning narrative. The battlecard is a collection of facts without a story. |
| 1 | Positioning is contradictory, confusing, or missing. |

### 10. Completeness

Are all required sections populated with real content?

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 5 | Every section is populated. No "TBD." Pricing intel, red flags, and quick win plays are all present. |
| 4 | All major sections populated. 1-2 minor sections may be thin. |
| 3 | Most sections populated but 2-3 are clearly underdeveloped or placeholder content. |
| 2 | Several sections are skeletal. Key areas like pricing or trap questions are missing. |
| 1 | Half or more sections are empty, TBD, or boilerplate. |

## Scoring Thresholds

| Total Score | Verdict | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **40-50** | **Ship it.** This battlecard is ready for the sales team. | Present to user. Note any `[VERIFY]` flags for follow-up. |
| **25-39** | **Needs revision.** Solid foundation but specific dimensions need work. | Identify the lowest-scoring dimensions and revise before presenting. |
| **Below 25** | **Start over.** Not enough intel or too many quality issues to patch. | Return to Phase 1 for more intel gathering. Tell the user what's needed. |

## Hard Fail Rules

Regardless of total score, the battlecard fails if:
- **Any dimension scores a 1.** A single catastrophic weakness undermines the whole card.
- **Evidence Density is below 3.** An unsupported battlecard is worse than no battlecard.
- **Competitive Honesty is below 3.** Reps who discover you lied about competitor weaknesses will never trust your content again.
