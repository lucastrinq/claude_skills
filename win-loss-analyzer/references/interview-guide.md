# Win/Loss Interview Guide

How to design, conduct, and code win/loss interviews that produce actionable intelligence.

## Pre-Interview Prep

**Before every interview:**
- [ ] Review the CRM record: deal size, timeline, competitors involved, stage progression, rep notes
- [ ] Identify 2-3 specific hypotheses to test (e.g., "We think we lost on pricing" — verify or disprove)
- [ ] Know who you're talking to: their role in the decision, how long they've been at the company
- [ ] Prepare role-specific angles (a VP cares about different things than an end user)
- [ ] Send a brief agenda: "15-20 minutes, just want to understand your evaluation process to help us improve"
- [ ] Decide: won deal or lost deal interview? The framing changes significantly.

## Interview Structure

### Opening (2 minutes)

Set the tone: honest, non-defensive, genuinely curious.

**Say this:** "Thanks for taking the time. We're trying to improve, so I want to understand your experience evaluating us — what worked, what didn't, and what ultimately drove your decision. There are no wrong answers, and candid feedback is the most helpful thing you can give us."

**Do NOT say:** "We'd love your feedback on our product" (leads to politeness, not honesty).

### Decision Context (3-4 minutes)

Understand the landscape before diving into specifics.

1. **What triggered the evaluation?** "What was happening in your business that made you start looking for a solution?"
   - *Listen for:* urgency level, pain severity, whether this was top-down or bottom-up
   - *Follow-up:* "How long had that been a problem before you started looking?"

2. **Who was involved in the decision?** "Walk me through who was part of the evaluation and what each person cared about."
   - *Listen for:* buying committee composition, conflicting priorities, hidden influencers
   - *Follow-up:* "Was there anyone who had strong feelings one way or another?"

3. **What alternatives did you consider?** "Besides us, what other options were on the table — including doing nothing?"
   - *Listen for:* which competitors, how serious each was, whether status quo was a real option
   - *Follow-up:* "How did you narrow it down?"

### Evaluation Process (5-7 minutes)

Understand how they evaluated and what mattered.

4. **What were your must-haves?** "What were the non-negotiable requirements any solution had to meet?"
   - *Listen for:* decision criteria hierarchy, deal-breakers
   - *Follow-up:* "Were those the same at the start and end, or did they shift?"

5. **What was your experience with our product?** "What stood out — positively or negatively — when you evaluated us?"
   - *Listen for:* specific moments, demo reactions, trial experience
   - *Follow-up:* "Can you give me a specific example?"

6. **How did we compare to alternatives?** "When you compared us side by side with [competitor], where did we stand out and where did we fall short?"
   - *Listen for:* specific comparison points, not generalities
   - *Follow-up:* "Was that a dealbreaker or more of a nice-to-have?"

7. **How was working with our team?** "What was your experience with our sales team, support, and the process overall?"
   - *Listen for:* responsiveness, understanding their needs, helpfulness vs. pushiness
   - *Follow-up:* "Was there anything that would have changed your experience?"

### Decision Drivers (3-4 minutes)

Get to the real reasons.

8. **What ultimately drove the decision?** "When it came down to it, what was the single biggest factor in your decision?"
   - *Listen for:* the REAL reason, not the polite reason
   - *Follow-up:* "Was that the reason everyone agreed on, or were there different perspectives?"

9. **[For losses] What would have changed the outcome?** "If you could change one thing about our product or process, what would have made you choose us?"
   - *Listen for:* actionable gaps vs. fundamental misfit
   - *Follow-up:* "Is that something that would make you reconsider in the future?"

10. **[For wins] What almost stopped you?** "Was there a moment where you almost went a different direction?"
    - *Listen for:* near-misses, hidden objections that were overcome, lucky breaks
    - *Follow-up:* "What convinced you to stay with us at that point?"

### Competitive Intelligence (2-3 minutes)

If a competitor was involved, dig in.

11. **What did [competitor] do well?** "What was the strongest part of their pitch or product?"
    - *Listen for:* honest assessment, features/capabilities, experience quality
    - *Follow-up:* "Was that something we should be doing?"

12. **What did [competitor] do poorly?** "Where did they fall short in your evaluation?"
    - *Listen for:* weaknesses, implementation concerns, trust issues

13. **How did pricing compare?** "Without sharing specifics, how did the pricing models compare? Was price a major factor?"
    - *Listen for:* relative positioning, value perception, budget constraints

### Wrap-Up (2 minutes)

14. **Anything else?** "Is there anything I should have asked that I didn't?"
    - Often produces the most honest insights — people share what's really on their mind.

15. **[For losses] Would you reconsider?** "Under what circumstances would you evaluate us again?"
    - *Listen for:* renewal timing, feature triggers, competitive dissatisfaction
    - *Follow-up:* "Would you be open to a check-in in [timeframe]?"

## What to Listen For (Beyond Words)

| Signal | What It Might Mean |
|---|---|
| Hesitation before answering "why you chose them" | The stated reason may not be the real one |
| "It wasn't just one thing" | Multiple small issues compounded — no single fix |
| Praising your product but still losing | Sales execution or process issue, not product |
| "Your rep was great, but..." | They felt sold to, not consulted |
| Vague answers about competitor evaluation | They're being polite — push gently for specifics |
| Enthusiasm about a specific feature | Potential win theme — validate across other interviews |
| "We'd consider you again when..." | Real buying signal for pipeline — flag for sales |

## Handling Difficult Responses

| Situation | Response |
|---|---|
| They're being too polite | "I really appreciate that, but the most helpful thing you can do is be direct. What would you tell your colleague about us over coffee?" |
| They're vague on pricing | "I'm not asking for exact numbers — just generally, was pricing a significant factor or was it more about capabilities?" |
| They blame internal politics | "That makes sense. If the internal dynamics had been different, do you think the outcome would have changed?" |
| They don't remember details | "That's totally fine. What's the one thing that sticks out most from the evaluation?" |
| They're angry/frustrated | "I hear you, and I'm sorry about that experience. Can you help me understand specifically what went wrong so we can fix it?" |

## Post-Interview Coding Template

For each interview, capture:

```
Interview #:
Date:
Contact: [Name, Role, Company]
Deal: [Size, Segment, Outcome]
Competitors: [Who else was evaluated]

Primary loss/win reason: [One sentence]
Root cause category: [From loss taxonomy]
Confidence in categorization: [High/Medium/Low]

Key quotes: [2-3 verbatim quotes]

Product feedback:
- [Feature/capability mentioned] — [blocker/nice-to-have] — [competitor has this? Y/N]

Competitive intel:
- [What they said about competitor]

Sales process feedback:
- [What went well/poorly in our process]

Action items:
- [Specific follow-up needed]
```
